Supported by the Brady Education Foundation and led by NCDPI's Office of Research and Promising Practices in partnership with research colleagues at NC State University.

A strength-based approach

Start with what is already working.

Most improvement efforts begin by asking what is going wrong. The Bright Spots approach adds a different question: where are schools improving despite similar constraints, and what can we learn from what they are doing?

Which schools are improving despite similar challenges, what are they doing differently, and what can other schools adapt?

The pilot uses a positive-deviance approach. The goal is not to copy another school's program. Participating teams study concrete practices, decide what fits their students and community, and then test and refine those practices through the school year.

How the pilot works

Learn. Design. Test. Improve. Share.

The pilot turns the Bright Spots cycle into a supported school-improvement process.

1

Learn from Bright Spots

Study specific routines used by schools that have substantially reduced chronic absenteeism in comparable settings.

2

Develop an action plan

Use the kickoff to select a focused set of practices, assign responsibilities, and establish clear measures of progress.

3

See practices in action

Engage in structured peer learning and school-based fieldwork to better understand how promising routines operate in practice.

4

Test and adjust

Implement the plan, review attendance and implementation data, and refine the approach through short improvement cycles.

5

Share what we learn

Document practical lessons, tools, and conditions that can inform future Bright Spots learning networks across North Carolina.

What participating schools receive

Support designed for implementation.

Action-planning support

A facilitated process for developing a focused, school-specific Attendance Action Plan.

Bright Spot peer learning

Direct access to educators in schools that have demonstrated substantial attendance improvement.

Coaching and problem solving

Regular check-ins to review progress, troubleshoot barriers, and refine implementation.

Data and progress monitoring

Support using attendance and implementation data to determine whether new routines are taking hold.

Practical tools

Templates, trackers, protocols, and other materials schools can adapt to existing systems.

Professional learning support

Resources supporting implementation, including eligible stipends associated with the planned weekend kickoff.

What participation involves

A small cohort with meaningful support.

Participating implementation schools should be prepared to make attendance a focused improvement priority during the 2026–27 school year.

  • ✓Identify a cross-role school attendance team.
  • ✓Participate in the full-day kickoff and action-planning session.
  • ✓Develop and implement a school-specific Attendance Action Plan.
  • ✓Participate in regular project check-ins and periodic progress reviews.
  • ✓Engage in peer learning with Bright Spot schools.
  • ✓Use attendance and implementation data to test and refine practices.
  • ✓Participate in research and feedback activities focused on feasibility, usefulness, and sustainability.

Why only four implementation schools?

This is intentionally designed as a small feasibility study. Limiting the initial cohort allows the project team to provide close implementation support, study how the model works in practice, and refine it before broader expansion.

Important: Participation in broader AttendNC activities does not automatically include the ongoing coaching and implementation support reserved for the four-study-school cohort.

Related, but separate

Two attendance pilots. Different purposes.

NCDPI is supporting two separate attendance efforts during 2026–27. Districts may encounter both, but they have different goals, structures, and selection processes.

Brady-supported study

Bright Spots Learning Pilot

A small research and implementation effort focused on learning from Bright Spot schools and supporting four implementation schools through action planning, peer learning, coaching, and improvement cycles.

State-funded pilot

Student Attendance Early Intervention Pilot Program

A separate legislative initiative in which NCDPI will select one PSU and participating schools to implement a data-driven attendance intervention system with a selected vendor. It has its own PSU application and selection process.
