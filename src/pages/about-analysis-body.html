About the Analysis

Bright Spots are identified by asking a context-sensitive question: which schools have chronic absenteeism rates that are substantially lower than we would expect given the circumstances in which they operate?

The basic idea

Compare schools with what the data predict for their context.

Simply identifying schools with the lowest chronic absenteeism rates would favor schools serving easier-to-attend contexts. The Bright Spots analysis instead estimates an expected chronic absenteeism rate using school and socioeconomic context, then examines how far each school’s actual rate falls above or below that expectation.

1. School contextUse statewide school and socioeconomic information to establish an expected attendance context.
2. Predicted rateEstimate the chronic absenteeism rate expected for a school with that context.
3. Actual vs. predictedCompare the school’s observed rate with the model prediction.
4. Bright Spot signalIdentify schools whose actual rate is substantially lower than predicted.

Bright Spot classifications

How strong is the positive deviation?

The map groups schools by how far their chronic absenteeism rate falls below the model prediction, expressed in standard-deviation units.

Promising

1.25–1.5 SDs below

A meaningful positive deviation that warrants attention and may merit closer inquiry.

Strong

1.5–2 SDs below

A larger positive deviation from the model-predicted chronic absenteeism rate.

Exceptional

≥ 2 SDs below

The most pronounced positive deviations identified in the statewide screen.

Interpretation

What the analysis can and cannot tell us.

What it can tell us

  • Which schools are performing substantially better than predicted on chronic absenteeism given their context.
  • Where positive anomalies appear across regions and grade bands.
  • Which schools may be especially useful sites for deeper qualitative inquiry.

What it cannot establish

  • That one school is universally “better” than another.
  • That a particular program or practice caused the attendance result.
  • That a strategy observed in one school will transfer unchanged to every other setting.
The residual is a signal for inquiry, not a causal effect or a ranking. Bright Spot identification tells us where to look. Interviews, school-level documentation, and subsequent testing are needed to understand what may be contributing to the result and what might transfer to similar schools.

From signal to practice

The analysis is the first step, not the endpoint.

AttendNC uses Bright Spot identification to guide positive-deviance inquiry. School interviews and fieldwork then surface concrete routines and enabling conditions that educators believe contribute to better attendance. Those lessons can be translated into strategies that other schools can adapt, test, monitor, and refine.
