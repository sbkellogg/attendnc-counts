A strength-based approach

Start with what is working.

Bright Spots flips the usual improvement question. Instead of asking only why attendance is poor, we ask where schools are improving despite similar constraints, what they are doing differently, and what others can adapt.

AttendNC Bright Spots is designed to connect data, fieldwork, and practical improvement. Statewide attendance trends identify schools whose progress deserves a closer look. Interviews and school visits then surface the practices, routines, and enabling conditions behind that progress.

The goal is not to name a single “best” intervention. It is to identify patterns that schools can test in their own context, while preserving the local knowledge and judgment of educators, students, and families.

The common thread is not a single program. It is a consistent way of noticing, understanding, responding, and following through.

How the Bright Spots cycle works

  1. DetermineUse data to find schools significantly reducing absenteeism for their context.
  2. DiscoverLearn directly from school teams about uncommon but successful practices.
  3. DesignTranslate local insights into practical strategies and action plans.
  4. DiscernTest, monitor, and refine strategies using ongoing data and feedback.
  5. DisseminateShare promising practices so similar schools can adapt what fits.

What we’re learning

Different schools. Different strategies. Consistent ways of working.

Early interviews across elementary, middle, high school, and lab-school settings point to several recurring themes.

Relationships & relevance

Make school worth attending.

Students are more likely to attend when they are known, connected, and can see the relevance of school to their relationships, interests, goals, and future opportunities.

Data, teams & follow-through

Notice early and act together.

Bright Spot schools tend to review attendance frequently, share responsibility across roles, assign next steps clearly, and follow through before absences accumulate.

Match response to reason

Understand before intervening.

Transportation, housing, anxiety, family routines, peer conflict, work, and disengagement require different responses. The number of absences is a signal, not the diagnosis.

A practical Bright Spots sequence: notice early, understand why, act together, create connections, and follow through.
Notice earlyIdentify students before absences accumulate.
Understand whyTalk with students and families before choosing a response.
Act togetherMake attendance a team responsibility.
Create connectionsBuild belonging and relevant opportunities.
Follow throughAssign responsibility, act, monitor, and adjust.

Promising-practice profiles, not impact evaluations

Bright Spot selection tells us which schools merit closer inquiry. Interviews identify plausible practices and enabling conditions that may help explain improvement, but they do not establish that any one practice caused the change.
