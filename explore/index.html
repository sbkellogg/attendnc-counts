Find schools making unusual progress.

How to use the map

See what each school's numbers mean.

Filter by Bright Spot strength and grade band, then click a school marker to see the rates and improvement measures behind its designation.

Chronic absenteeism rates

  • Peak rate (2021–22): the school's chronic absenteeism rate during the statewide pandemic-era peak.
  • Current rate (2024–25): the school's most recent chronic absenteeism rate.
  • Model-expected rate: the rate predicted for a school with similar characteristics.

Improvement measures

  • Improvement (pp): the percentage-point reduction since 2021–22.
  • % reduction from peak: the relative decline from peak to current rate.
  • Difference vs. expected (pp): how far below the model-expected rate the school's 2024–25 rate falls.
  • Model residual: a standardized measure of how strongly a school is outperforming similar schools.
Interpret the map as a discovery tool, not a scorecard. Together these measures distinguish schools that improved substantially in absolute terms from schools that are outperforming expectations after accounting for context.

Narrow by school context

  • County: limit results to a selected county.
  • Grade band: filter by elementary, middle, high, or other configurations.
  • Enrollment: limit results by school size.
  • Poverty designation: narrow by whether a school is identified as higher poverty.
  • Share of economically disadvantaged students: filter by the percentage of students identified as economically disadvantaged.

Narrow by Bright Spot strength

  • Bright Spot category: filter by Exceptional, Strong, or Promising.
  • Decrease since the 2021–22 peak: narrow by how much a school's rate has declined.
  • School inclusion: view Bright Spots only, or include all schools for broader comparison.

From signal to practice

Move from a statewide view to a focused set of schools.

Start by narrowing the map and table to a comparable group of schools, then use a set of guiding questions to structure a learning conversation once you've identified schools of interest.

Who is improving in a context like ours?

Use the county, grade band, enrollment, poverty designation, and economically disadvantaged student filters to narrow to comparable schools.

Which schools are the clearest Bright Spots?

Use the Exceptional, Strong, and Promising categories to focus on schools outperforming model expectations by different degrees.

Where should we learn more?

Use the table to identify a small set of schools worth contacting, studying, or inviting into a peer-learning conversation.

What systems changed?

Consider attendance teams, early warning routines, outreach cadence, and how staff monitored progress.

Which barriers were reduced?

Consider transportation, health, scheduling, family supports, or other local challenges affecting attendance.

3

How did belonging and engagement grow?

Consider peer connections, adult mentorship, climate routines, and student voice.

4

What did leaders do to support the work?

Consider staff roles, follow-through, communication routines, and how the work was sustained over time.

Use responsibly

A tool for inquiry, not for labeling.

The model accounts for structural factors, but it cannot identify the specific practices, leadership decisions, or community conditions that drove improvement. Bright Spot identification should be followed by local validation, including conversations with school staff, students, families, and district leaders.

This analysis is...

  • A way to identify schools with meaningful attendance gains relative to context.
  • A starting point for inquiry, site visits, and peer learning.
  • A tool to help districts and schools ask what changed, what stayed consistent, and what adults did differently.

This analysis is not...

  • A causal claim about why a school improved.
  • A ranking of the “best” schools.
  • Proof that a specific program or practice will work everywhere.
